Do home batteries actually pay for themselves in Melbourne?

Short answer: it depends on when you use power, not just how much.

A battery saves you the gap between what you're paid to export solar (a few cents) and what you pay to buy power back after 4pm. In most Melbourne homes that gap is the biggest line on the bill. Households that are empty all day and busy at night get the most out of storage, because almost everything they use is bought at peak rates. If someone is home during the day using solar directly, the payback stretches out. Send us a recent bill and we'll show you which of the two you are before you spend anything.

Victorian solar and battery rebates in 2026: what's on offer

Federal and state programs stack, and the rules move every year.

There are usually two layers: a federal discount applied at the point of sale, and Victorian programs that change with each budget. Eligibility normally turns on your property value, household income, whether you own the home, and whether the property has claimed a rebate before. The amounts and cut-off dates shift, so anything you read from last year is probably out of date. We check what you currently qualify for, take it off the quote, and lodge the forms ourselves so you're not chasing paperwork.

How big a battery does a Melbourne home need?

Most homes land between 10 and 15 kWh. Here's how to tell.

Look at your evening and overnight usage rather than your total. That's the part a battery actually covers. A typical three-bedroom home uses somewhere between 8 and 14 kWh between 4pm and 7am, so a 10 to 15 kWh battery covers most nights without paying for capacity that sits idle. Going bigger makes sense if you have a pool, ducted heating, or an electric car charging at home. Going smaller can work if you just want to shave the peak. Stackable systems let you add capacity later.

Can you add a battery to solar you already have?

Usually yes — and there are two ways to do it.

If your existing inverter is healthy, an AC coupled battery sits alongside it with its own inverter. Nothing on your roof changes and the original system keeps working. If your inverter is near the end of its life, replacing it with a hybrid unit is often better value, because one box then handles both the panels and the battery. Which is cheaper over ten years depends on the age and brand of what you have now, so we check the switchboard and inverter before quoting either.

10 July 2026

Running air conditioning on solar: what it really costs

The most expensive appliance in a Melbourne home can become one of the cheapest.

A modern split system draws roughly 1 to 2 kW while it's working hard. On a hot afternoon a 6.6 kW solar system is making far more than that, so cooling is close to free while the sun is up. The cost shows up on summer evenings and winter mornings, when the panels are quiet and the unit is still running. That's exactly the window a battery covers. It's also why sizing the air conditioner properly matters: an oversized unit short-cycles and uses more power than a right-sized one.

26 June 2026

Blackout backup: what a battery can and can't do

Most batteries switch off in an outage unless they're wired for it.

For safety, a grid-connected system shuts down during a blackout so it can't send power into lines that someone may be working on. To keep your house running you need a backup circuit installed, which separates chosen circuits from the grid and lets the battery feed them. You choose what goes on it — usually the fridge, lights, internet and a couple of power points, rather than the whole house. Say up front that backup matters to you, because it changes both the design and the price.
